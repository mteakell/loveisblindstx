# What to send the owners

Copy the message below into an email or text. Replace the URL once the site is live.

---

**Subject: Two minutes after an install, and I can rank you for it**

Quick ask, and it should genuinely take two minutes.

Google has stopped rewarding pages that repeat what everyone else already wrote. It now
favours pages that add something new. The three of you are sitting on the only thing on
this site that nobody else in Texas can publish: what actually happens on a job.

So here is the link. Bookmark it on your phone:

**loveisblindstx.com/job-notes**

After an install, open it and fill in whatever you can be bothered to type. Only two boxes
are required, your name and the city. Everything else is optional. One good sentence beats
a blank form.

The bit I want most is this one: **what did they ask for, and what did you actually
recommend instead, and why.** That gap is where all the useful content lives. "They wanted
blackout in the living room, I put solar shades in because it is a west wall and they
wanted to keep the view" is a blog post. I cannot invent that. Nobody can research it.

Second most useful: **the subdivision or street.** If you tell me the older section of
Grapevine has frames that are never square, or that a particular builder in Celina used a
frame depth too shallow for shutters, that is a fact that exists nowhere else on the
internet. It is the kind of thing that makes Google treat you as the local expert rather
than another blinds site.

**Would rather talk than type?** Record a voice memo on the drive to the next job and send
it to me. Same value, no typing. Honestly this is the easiest way to do it.

**Photos.** A phone shot of a finished install beats stock photography every time, and
stock is what most competitors use. Text them over or drop them in the shared album.

Two rules: no customer names or addresses unless they have said yes, and do not worry
about writing it well. That is my job. I just need the facts.

If you have five minutes rather than two, the page has a list of questions at the bottom.
Pick any one and answer it in a couple of sentences.

## What each field is actually for

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Subdivision or neighbourhood | Hyper-local facts nobody else publishes. The single highest-value field. |
| What they asked for first | The gap between what people want and what they need is a whole content category. |
| What you recommended, and why | This is expertise. It cannot be researched, only observed. |
| What you solved on site | Real problems and real fixes are what make a how-to credible. |
| What surprised the customer | Customer surprise marks a genuine information gap in the market. |
| Window orientation | Ties every recommendation to a verifiable physical reason. |
| Photos | Original imagery is the strongest originality signal and cannot be scraped. |

## Realistic cadence

Do not ask for a submission per job. Ask for **one per week per owner**, and take whatever
you get. Three owners at one a week is around 150 submissions a year, which is far more raw
material than you can turn into posts.

If adoption stalls, the fix is almost always the voice memo route rather than more
reminders. Installers will talk for two minutes and will not type for two minutes.

## What to do with what comes back

- **Subdivision or builder facts** go into the relevant city page, not a blog post. They
  make the local pages genuinely unique, which is what the city pages currently lack.
- **The asked-for versus recommended gap** becomes comparison posts, which is a cluster
  already targeted in the 30 new posts.
- **Solved-on-site notes** go into the measuring and installation posts as real examples.
- **Photos** replace the library images on city and product pages.

## One thing worth knowing

Everything on the site right now is accurate but generic, because generic is all that could
be written without you. The pages that will outrank competitors are the ones carrying facts
that came from a van, not from a keyword tool.
