Window treatments for Magnolia homes

Magnolia buyers usually want two things from a window treatment: keep the view of the land, and stop the heat that comes with it. On an open lot nothing shades the west side of the house, so the late sun reaches straight into living rooms and across back porches.

That is why most Magnolia consultations start at the back of the house. We look at which windows and porch openings face west, how far you want to see out, and which rooms need darkness at night, then bring fabric samples you can hold up against your own glass.

Roller and solar shades

Keep the view, lose the glare

A solar screen roller shade is the usual answer for big windows that look over land. The weave cuts glare and UV at the glass while you still see the yard, the fence line and the trees. A tighter weave goes on the west windows and a more open one on the north and east, so each room keeps as much view as it can.

Bedrooms that face the same view need a second plan for night. A light filtering or blackout fabric, or a dual shade with a darkening layer, gives you privacy after dark without covering the window all day.

Exterior patio shades

Back porches that stay usable in July

A covered porch blocks the midday sun but not the low afternoon sun that slides in under the roof. Exterior patio shades hang from the porch header and stop that sun before it reaches the chairs. Retractable versions roll up into a housing when you want the porch open, and motorized ones raise with a button when the weather turns.

On a lot with no neighbors close by, most people pick a weave that keeps the view across the property rather than a tight privacy fabric.

See exterior patio shades

Woven wood shades

Texture for rooms that face the trees

Rooms on the shaded side of the lot often need warmth more than sun control. Woven wood shades in bamboo or natural grasses filter the light into a soft glow and suit the farmhouse and ranch styles common out here. Add a privacy or blackout liner where the room needs it at night.

Questions

Magnolia window treatment questions

Do you come out to homes on acreage around Magnolia?

Yes. The consultation is at your home, wherever it is in the Magnolia area, and it is free. We measure every window and porch opening on site.

What is the best shade for a west-facing window in Magnolia?

Usually a solar screen roller shade with a tighter weave. It cuts the afternoon glare and heat at the glass while keeping the view. Rooms that also need darkness at night get a blackout or dual shade.

Can patio shades go on a porch that faces open land?

Yes. Exterior patio shades mount to the porch header, with track-guided options for openings that catch wind. Retractable shades roll up out of the weather when you are not using the porch.

Will woven wood shades give privacy at night?

Unlined, only partly, because the weave has natural gaps. With a privacy or blackout liner they cover like any other shade.

Can you shade a detached shop or outdoor kitchen too?

Exterior patio shades mount to the header of a covered opening, so an outdoor kitchen or covered structure can be measured the same way as a porch.

How much do window treatments cost in Magnolia?

It depends on the size of each opening, the material and the options you choose. We measure every opening on site and give you a written quote with installation included. The consultation is free.
